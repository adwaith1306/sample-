Supernatural and Gothic horror are distinct but closely linked subgenres within the broader realm of horror.
Supernatural horror revolves around elements beyond the natural world, incorporating paranormal entities such as ghosts, demons, witches, vampires, or werewolves. It delves is rooted in eerie, atmospheric settings like ancient castles, crumbling mansions, or isolated estates. It blends macabre themes with romance and melodrama, often exploring decay, death, and the supernatural, with an emphasis on evocative architecture and haunting environments to build suspense and dread. Both subgenres effectively tap into primal fears, challenging emotional thresholds and immersing audiences in dark, unsettling worlds. Gothic horror is a literary and artistic genre that emerged during the 18th century, blending elements of terror, romance, and the supernatural.
